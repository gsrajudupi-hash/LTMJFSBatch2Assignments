===========Additional Database Design Challenges=======================

1)	Can one order contain food from multiple restaurants? How would you redesign the schema?
A)	The current design supports one restaurant per order. To support food items from multiple restaurants in one order, we can create a restaurant_order table between orders and restaurant. Each restaurant can then process its part of the order separately.

2)	How will you maintain multiple payment attempts for a single order?
A)	For multiple payment attempts on the same order, allow multiple payment records for one order_id. Each payment record can store its own reference_id, payment status, payment type, amount, and timestamp.
	Design change: remove the UNIQUE constraint from payment.order_id.
	Order -> Payement = 1:M
	
3)  How will you preserve historical food prices?
A)	Store the food price at the time of ordering in order_item.item_price. Even if food_item.food_price changes later, old orders will still retain the original price paid by the customer.

4)	How will you maintain cancellation reasons and refund details?
A)	Store the cancellation status and timestamp in order_status_history. Store refund details such as refund amount, status, reason, date, and reference ID in the refund table linked to the corresponding payment.
This preserves both the order cancellation history and refund transaction details.

5)	How will you maintain the complete delivery status history?
A)	Use order_status_history to store each delivery/order status change as a separate row, along with status_updated_at.

6)	How will you restrict customers from using the same coupon beyond the allowed limit?
A)	Use the coupon_redemption table to track how many times each customer has used a coupon. Before applying a coupon, check the customer's redemption count against the allowed usage limit, and reject the coupon when the limit is reached.

7)	How will you prevent orders when restaurants are closed?
A)	Before placing an order, check the restaurant's restaurant_active, start_time, and end_time. The application should allow the order only when the restaurant is ACTIVE and the current time is within its operating hours.

8)	How will you handle food items that become unavailable after being added to a cart?
A)	Before checkout, recheck the food_item.availability status for every item in the cart. If an item becomes unavailable, prevent that item from being ordered and notify the customer to remove or replace it.

9)	How will you ensure consistent order placement when multiple customers order simultaneously?
A)	Use database transactions to ensure all order-related operations are completed consistently. Creating the order, adding order_item records, validating availability, and processing related updates should succeed together or be rolled back if any step fails.

10)	 What happens to historical orders when a customer requests account deletion?
A)	 Historical orders should not be deleted, because they are required for transaction, payment, refund, and reporting history. Instead, use soft deletion/anonymization by marking the customer account as deleted/inactive and removing or masking personal information while retaining the historical order records.
